A heuristic is useful to a search only insofar as it orders the states that search compares. Wilt & Ruml (2016) measure this as Goal Distance Rank Correlation: a single Kendall τ against exact distance-to-goal, pooled over every pair of states. We measure it stratiﬁed by distance instead, against exhaustive ground truth, on Rubik’s-cube sub-problems spanning 5.5 × 102 to 3.1 × 1023 states. Call the set of states at exact distance d from the goal the shell at d. A search compares adjacent shells, and we ﬁnd that a heuristic’s accuracy at that comparison is Φ(∆/(σ √ 2)) — where ∆ is the diﬀerence in mean heuristic value between the two shells, σ the spread of values within them, and Φ the standard normal distribution function — with no ﬁtted parameters. Estimating the moments and measuring accuracy on disjoint halves of each shell gives a mean absolute error of 0.012 over 252 shell observations, against an estimator noise ﬂoor of 0.002. It holds on a sub-problem 300 times larger whose ground truth comes from a diﬀerent apparatus, and on heuristics trained under three further objectives. Injecting noise of known tail weight degrades it monotonically, and Cauchy noise, which has no ﬁnite variance, breaks it by a factor of 36 — so the normal form, not mere monotonicity, carries the prediction. Three consequences follow. Ordering quality is invariant to rescaling. Decay with distance occurs exactly when σ grows, which is why it is not particular to learned heuristics: pattern databases on the same states decay as much, and matched on strength the two classes are indistinguishable. And σ is the one term an objective can act on — but every objective we built to act on it produced a worse heuristic, monotonically in the penalty weight. We also show that τ -b rewards a heuristic for being coarse-valued, by enough ( 0.084) to reverse the learned-versus-classical comparisons this statistic is used to make. The identity is classical; the measurement against exact ground truth is our contribution. We report three claims made by earlier versions of this work that our own controls subsequently refuted.
